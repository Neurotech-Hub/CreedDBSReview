\section{3. Electrode impedance and stimulation robustness}\label{electrode-impedance-and-stimulation-robustness}

A constant-current stimulator regulates current only while the voltage the electrode requires stays within its compliance, so the electrode load determines how much of the programmable range is usable.

\subsection{3.2 In-vivo impedance measurement}\label{in-vivo-impedance-measurement}

Platinum electrodes were implanted bilaterally in four mice across two surgical cohorts (eight electrodes), and impedance was measured weekly from 1 to 8 weeks after implantation. At each session a StimJim stimulator \citep{cermak2019} delivered 10 µA, 450 µs pulses at 130 Hz for 10 s through each electrode against the common return, and the pulse voltage and delivered current reported by the StimJim were recorded five times. Impedance was computed as voltage divided by delivered current and averaged over the five readings; this quantity is an effective, pulse-derived estimate of the load, referred to as impedance in this section. This two-electrode configuration is the one the stimulator itself drives. Two- and three-electrode impedance spectroscopy measurements are highly correlated \citep{koo2018}, which supports the two-electrode configuration but does not validate the time-domain pulse estimate used here specifically. This pulse-based estimate matches the quantity that matters for a constant-current stimulator, the voltage required per unit of delivered current, rather than a small-signal impedance at a single frequency.

Three electrodes were excluded. One read near 0 V at every session, consistent with a short to the return. Both electrodes of one mouse gave unstable readings, varying by up to 4.7-fold between sessions and drifting within a session; whether this reflected the electrodes, the implant strategy or the recording setup could not be determined. The analyzed data set therefore comprises five electrodes from three mice, measured at eight weekly sessions (40 electrode-sessions).

Two properties of the measurement bound its interpretation. First, in a bench check against resistive loads, the voltage reported by the StimJim fell 6--73 \% below the oscilloscope-measured voltage for loads of 2--68 kΩ, with the largest errors above 10 kΩ, which would bias the in-vivo values low. Second, the 450 µs measurement pulse is longer than typical DBS pulses, so the polarization it includes would bias the values high relative to the load seen by shorter pulses. Because these biases act in opposite directions, the direction of the net error is unknown, and the values below are best treated as an estimate of the load range rather than a calibrated impedance.

\subsection{3.3 In-vivo impedance and the compliance envelope}\label{in-vivo-impedance-and-the-compliance-envelope}

Across the five electrodes, impedance ranged from 32 to 80 kΩ (49.1 ± 9.9 kΩ, mean ± SD over all electrode-sessions). The weekly mean was 43.0 ± 7.4 kΩ at 1 week, peaked at 54.2 ± 14.8 kΩ at 3 weeks and returned to 44.6 ± 7.5 kΩ by 8 weeks, remaining within 43--54 kΩ throughout (Figure 4A). Mean impedance of individual electrodes ranged from 42.7 to 64.2 kΩ, so differences between electrodes (about 1.5-fold) were larger than the change in the group mean over time.

These loads place FLEX-DBS in its compliance-limited regime. With an output swing of about 4.6 V, measured into 47 kΩ, and 2.66 kΩ of sense and filter resistance in series with the electrode, the largest regulated current is approximately $I_\mathrm{max} = 4.6\ \mathrm{V} / (|Z| + 2.66\ \mathrm{k}\Omega)$ (Figure 4B). The weekly mean impedances correspond to 81--101 µA, and individual electrode-sessions to 56--131 µA, compared with the 600 µA nominal range of the application. The 100 µA reference protocol was within compliance in 11 of 40 electrode-sessions, and in 0 to 4 of the 8 sessions for individual electrodes. A battery-direct design with $\sim$3 V of compliance would reach only $\sim$38--93 µA into the same loads. The usable amplitude is set by electrode impedance and compliance together, not by the programmable range, and it differs between electrodes more than it changes over time after implantation.
